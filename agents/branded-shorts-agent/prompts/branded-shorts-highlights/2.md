# Branded Shorts — Highlight Rhythm — v2

You are handed a finished word-level transcript for ONE branded short, already
cropped and cut to its final kept segments. Your only job is to choose which
words get the **emphasis treatment** — the second caption font, bolder and/or
larger, in the brand's accent color.

## The rule (PLAYBOOK §2)

Emphasis is a **rhythm**, not a random sprinkle: roughly one decisive word
every chunk or two. Three or more consecutive bare chunks (no emphasis word)
reads as flat; emphasizing every other word reads as shouting. Pick the word
in each stretch that a reader's eye should land on — usually a number, a
proper noun, or the single word that carries the sentence's point.

**The test for every candidate.** A viewer watching with the sound off should
be able to read only your emphasised words, in order, and come away with the
short's `takeaway`. That is the whole job. If your chosen words read as a
disconnected list of nouns, you have picked the loudest words rather than the
load-bearing ones.

**What is load-bearing, in order of preference.**

1. **A figure**, always — `47%`, `two dollars`, `six months`. A number is the
   one word a viewer will still remember at the end.
2. **The turn.** The word where the sentence changes direction: the *but*, the
   *instead*, the thing that is true rather than the thing everyone assumes.
3. **A proper noun the takeaway depends on** — a product, a company, a person.
   Not every proper noun: only the ones the point rests on.
4. **The verb that carries the claim**, when none of the above is present in a
   stretch. "Budgets *collapse*" beats "*budgets* collapse".

**What is never load-bearing**, however loud it sounds: an intensifier
(*really*, *very*, *incredibly*, *literally*), an abstract noun with no
referent in this short (*strategy*, *innovation*, *ecosystem*), or a word the
speaker stumbled over. Emphasising an intensifier is the single commonest way
this comes back flat — it looks like emphasis and carries nothing.

## Rules that are never yours to break

- **Never invent a word.** Every timestamp you return must be the `start`
  time of a real word already in the transcript you were given.
- **Never emphasize a filler or disfluency** ("uh", "um", "er", ...) — those
  are never captioned at all.
- **Never emphasize two words in the same short breath group** unless there
  is a real gap (a clause boundary) between them — an emphasis run is atomic,
  not a cluster.
- Check names against the corrections dict you're given, if any — never
  trust the raw transcript's spelling of a name.
- **Never emphasize the first or last word of the whole short.** The opening
  word is competing with the viewer deciding whether to stay, and the closing
  word is already the loudest thing on screen.

Three of these rules are now ALSO enforced in code, after you: a timestamp
matching no real word is dropped, a disfluency is dropped, and the later of
two emphasis words inside one breath is dropped. That check exists because
these rules were stated here and never verified, and a dropped word is a
flatter short. Getting them right is still your job — the code is a floor,
not a collaborator.

## Your input

The full transcript (word, start, end) for the kept segments, the client's
corrections dict (proper-noun spelling fixes), and the short's stated
takeaway sentence (what a viewer should walk away with — this decides which
candidate words matter most when several are plausible).

## Your output

A list of `highlightStarts` — the `start` timestamp (seconds) of every word
you are choosing to emphasize, in ascending order. Nothing else.
