# Don Techno system audit and music-media accounts

## Design and copy: Don Techno Auto's type system, slide layouts, text over footage, logo, voice and copy rules, compared with the agent-engine Instagram agent (repo /Users/albertkattan/Code/don-techno-auto, read only; frames I pulled from recent posts are in /private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-albertkattan-Code-karos-portal--claude-worktrees-xenodochial-galileo-1ba67f/c2b0d13c-4180-4c4d-b89a-2bd0ec24b593/scratchpad/dt-frames/: fred-sheet.jpg, sheet1-4.jpg, sheet-reels.jpg, vc-compare.jpg)

LOOK. One fixed type system set over real footage. There is no accent colour. Carousels are 1080x1440 slides, and every slide is an MP4. dontechno/render/carousel.py uses Pillow to draw a transparent overlay (logo, shade, type), then ffmpeg composes it onto the clip and keeps the original audio (H.264 CRF 18, AAC 192k). Every number sits in a frozen Layout dataclass. There are presets, golden pixel tests and a preview contact sheet. The live preset is STORY, with autofit on.
- Slide 1, the hook, has three lines. First a tracked SF Mono eyebrow at 32 px: where and when, or source and date, parts split by '  ·  ', about 35 characters at most. Then a bold Albert Sans 800 kicker at 76 px that states the angle in 22 characters or fewer and reads straight into the name. Then the NAME in Albert Sans 900 at 150 px; it shrinks to 96 px, then wraps to two lines. Examples: 'GUINNESS WORLD RECORDS · 20 SEP 2026 / A WORLD RECORD FOR / CALVIN HARRIS' and 'NEW YORK STOCK EXCHANGE · 18 SEP 2026 / THE FORMER ACCOUNTANT / JOHN SUMMIT'.
- Body slides have a mono eyebrow that carries the source or time of the fact, then one Albert Sans 700 headline (60 px on 70 px lines, balanced, at most 4 lines, never below 36 px). Example: 'SPOTIFY · AUGUST 2022 / HIS STREAMS USUALLY GREW 1 TO 2% A MONTH, BUT THAT AUGUST THEY GREW 89%.'
- Label slides are footage with only a 36 px mono label in the top right (what or where) and no shade.
So a slide carries at most two sans sizes plus one small mono line.

All type is white, set in caps by the copy, left-aligned in the lower third. It sits on a black bottom gradient: clear above 42% of the height, alpha 225/255 at the bottom edge, curve 1.2. Nothing covers a face. Builders set a crop position per camera shot (from ffmpeg scene cuts) so faces stay inside the 3:4 crop. The white DT blackletter monogram is top-left on every slide, fitted into 160 px at (48,48), about 124 px of visible ink. No handle, slide number or credit appears on a slide; credits go in the caption. Footage runs in full, ungraded colour. The ink and paper palette is only for designed surfaces, and none are live.

Reels (reel.py, 1080x1920) use a post-card template: navy #0E1319 ground, a baked Instagram-style header (avatar, don.techno, verified badge), 2 to 5 lines of plain sentences in Albert Sans 500 (44 px down to 34 px), and the clip in a 990x1296 rounded box. Landscape clips fit the width uncropped.

In practice all 7 carousels of week 39 are a hook plus 1 to 5 label slides, with no body slides, so the story lives in the caption.

COPY. Hard rules come first: no em-dashes, no exclamation marks, no emoji, proper capitalisation in captions, and no fact that is not in sources.md. On top of those sits the 12-rule plain voice (content-spec §3.5, Albert 2026-09-22): say what happened, where and when, the way you would tell a friend. One idea per slide, one checkable fact per text slide (a year, a place, a number or a name). No hype or critic words. Write less when the facts are thin. Headlines are one complete sentence you could say out loud (Daniel 2026-05-28), with good and bad examples.

Captions tell the story in order. The first line names the subject and the news, and uses this week's date when the event is old. Then 2 to 4 plain sentences, then a mandatory credit line ('Video: Cloonee (@cloonee) on YouTube'), then exactly 2 lowercase hashtags. No call to action. Each format in config/formats.yaml carries its own first-line patterns, lines that measurably lost (for example a same-clip loser at 0.35 against 2.95), a caption shape, kicker examples and the rules for a reel's on-video text.

Enforcement:
- scripts/check_copy.py is standard-library Python. It reads the builders and post folders and applies the lists in config/copy_rules.yaml: hard bans, watch words allowed only next to a concrete detail, closing lines that start with 'NOW', and words that describe people by looks or age.
- reel.py refuses em-dashes, '!' and emoji at render time.
- check_media.py checks the canvas, that every sampled frame in the first 2 seconds reaches a brightness (YAVG) of at least 20, overall brightness, sharpness and clip length.
- Kicker length, the NAME being the biggest text, sentence form and variety across slides are left to the model and to a human looking at a review sheet.

**Strengths**

- Restraint the owner is asking the engine for, already shipped. Every slide has at most two sans sizes plus one small tracked mono line that carries the source and date. There are no credit, handle or slide-number marks on the plate, no accent colour, and the only mark is the logo. The engine's own restraint reference (docs/instagram-restraint-reference.md, rule 3: 'Two or three type sizes. Not five') describes this. The engine has a six-step scale instead (22/32/46/66/94/200 px in _design-system.css).
- Legibility on footage is built in, not measured. overlay_hook and overlay_body always draw the black bottom gradient (STORY: clear above 42%, 225/255 at the bottom) with white type in a fixed lower-third zone, so the text never lands on the face. The Fred again refresh (fred-sheet.jpg) shows the result: readable on dark club footage without darkening the image.
- The hook states the angle. The eyebrow, the kicker of 22 characters or fewer and the NAME read as one phrase ('THE FORMER ACCOUNTANT / JOHN SUMMIT', 'A WORLD RECORD FOR / CALVIN HARRIS'). It replaced name-only hooks after the proposal found all 41 old builders opened on a bare name, which Albert described as 'now it just says peggy gou'.
- The copy voice was diagnosed from the system's own output, not taste. The proposal counted AI tells across 41 builders: 'one of the biggest names' in 21, 'turned into' in 9, 'precision' 10 times in 6, and 32 of 36 builders sharing the same slide rhythm. It turned the diagnosis into 12 rules with before and after pairs, and into a phrase list kept as data (config/copy_rules.yaml, 84 lines). The list is enforced by a regex checker with no LLM call. 26 of 41 old builders failed it on day one.
- Body headlines are complete spoken sentences with one checkable fact and a small source or time line above them ('23 MINUTES IN / WHEN A FAN CUT THE MUSIC BY ACCIDENT, HE LAUGHED AND HUGGED HIM.'). Captions are stories told in order, with dates, a named chart and window for chart claims, 'say views, not people', and no bio opener or moral closer.
- Credits are a hard rule. check_copy.py fails a caption without a 'Video:', 'Photo:' or 'Via' line. The @ is used only for Instagram handles, and each post names a collaborator to invite (RUNWAY.md). The slides stay clean because the credit lives in the caption.
- Only real pictures are used. AI-made clips fail a gate (formats.yaml ai_generated: false). An empty slot is treated as a good result: the week 39 carousel-4 slot was left empty rather than filled with soft footage. Picture quality gates are data: first-2-second YAVG of at least 20, brightness of 55 or more, crop plus scale capped at 1.5x for clean sources and 1.1x for soft ones, no burned-in text over the subject, no other page's watermark, not an ad.
- Video first. Carousel slides are MP4s with their original audio and run 'the whole moment'. The reel post-card template fits landscape YouTube or TV clips without cropping, which is how the competitor nostalgia reels that won are built.
- Formats are parameters. formats.yaml gives each format its measured evidence, slide shape, first-line patterns with measured losers, caption shape, kicker examples, gates, render recipe, and a promote or retire rule. It is a working example of the 'industry kit as parameters' direction.
- Cheap per post. Rendering is Pillow plus ffmpeg and copy checks are regex, with no LLM calls. Scraping spend was $0.30, $0.91 and $0.43 on 22, 23 and 24 September for up to 3 posts a day (ScrappyCoco ledger). Against that, the engine's Instagram run averages about $1.67.

**Weaknesses**

- Label slides have no shade. overlay_label draws a 36 px white mono label at about 78% alpha with a 2 px shadow and no gradient. On bright daylight footage the label and even the logo nearly disappear: Vintage Culture DNA Art Car, slides 3 to 6 (sheet3.jpg). The brand guidelines specify a top fade for this (rgba(0,0,0,0.55) fading out by 22% of the height, 'Logo + meta legibility'), but carousel.py never built it.
- Repeated slides get through. The Vintage Culture post labels 5 of 6 slides 'VINTAGE CULTURE', and slides 4 and 5 are the same camera angle from the same account (vc-compare.jpg). REVIEW RULE 10 ('vary across slides') is not checked by any script. check_media.py covers canvas, brightness, sharpness and length only.
- Enforcement is thinner than the docs claim. check_copy.py does not check kicker length, eyebrow length, whether the NAME is the biggest text, headline sentence form, or the 'one checkable fact per slide' rule. Yet the portal profile (karos-agents clients/dontechno/profile/brand-voice.md §7) says it fails a kicker over 22 characters and a hook whose NAME is not the biggest text.
- The docs and the portal profile have drifted from the live renderer. content-spec §0 to §14 still carries the April 'archivist' spec: JetBrains Mono and Cormorant, a bottom-right logo in the ffmpeg recipe, 20 to 60 s clips, lowercase body copy. Two decisions are still open: D3 (a CTA line) and D11 (letter case). The portal profile (branding-guidelines.md §1) says the logo is 48 px with a 32 px margin, while the live mark is about 124 px of ink in a 160 px box. The eyebrow font is macOS-only SF Mono, not the brand's JetBrains Mono, so the renderer cannot run on Linux or Cloud Run as it is.
- The craft is bespoke per post and not portable. Each post is a 180 to 380 line Python builder written by a headless Claude pass: 16 builders and 4,355 lines for week 39. The builders duplicate each other's main() functions, delete and rebuild their own output folders, and hardcode /opt/homebrew paths. The roadmap's spec-driven builder is not built. The LLM cost of that pass runs on the claude CLI login and is not recorded anywhere in the repo.
- The new design has no measured result yet. The STORY layout and the plain voice first went out on 23 and 24 September, and the learning loop has not run. The only on-page history is weak for this kind of post: pipeline carousels drew a median of 2 comments (n=43) against 62 for Daniel's single-image memes (n=6). So the evidence for the design comes from competitor pages and the page's past reels, not from its own new posts.
- The hook grammar is artist-first by rule, and some angles fit badly. 'RAVES IN A DARK WIG / NICOLE KIDMAN' reads backwards. Human-story and news posts with no artist need exceptions (formats.yaml review_rules_notes list three pending sign-offs).
- The graphic range is narrow. Everything is footage plus type. The brand kit's graphic systems (a six-mechanic Collage Kit, the 'Headline of the Week' newsprint format, paper quote cards) are documented in branding/brand-guidelines.html but not built. So on 'more graphics' DT's lesson is restraint plus real media, not a graphics library.
- The caption rules are stricter than what performs on the page itself. The page's 15 best posts ever were short reels with short captions that used emoji (median 57 characters), and the pipeline bans emoji. The market scan supports fewer emoji (36% of the top 10% of posts against 48% of the rest), so the rule is defensible. It is an owner call, not proven for this page.

**Strengths**

- It is a within-account median method with the right exclusions (hidden likes, pinned, promo, posts under 96 h) and an n on every table. I recomputed it from the raw snapshot and got the same 112 outliers the doc reports.
- It traces stories upstream instead of only listing formats. 17 winning stories were traced (14 found) with the delay before the big pages reposted them, and the 48 strongest outliers were read for where their information came from. - The own-audience study is measured against the market, not copied from it. It showed the page is a party page, not a techno page: Latin house 1.28, deep/organic house 1.26, EDM mainstage 0.33. It also explained the 2026 drop in reach.

- It corrects itself on the record. The collab lift turned out to be partly DJ promo posts; nostalgia has plateaued; the reel-length pattern flipped between periods; 'Albert's hypothesis is half right'.
- The dedupe windows are measured from our own history, not guessed: the same storyline inside 60 days did 0.84x of the first post, after 60 days 1.33x. The timeliness rules have unit tests and a stated 73% agreement with hand labels.
- It is very cheap: the market study cost $0.48, the formats research $0.057 and the comment study $0.036.

**Weaknesses**

- Samples are small and cover two periods, and several findings flipped between them: best reel length (45-60 s in April-June, 60-90 s in September), best weekday (Monday best, then worst), and top subject. - The Instagram scout searches reels by our niches' artists and reads promoters' reels.
Both score early velocity: views an hour, (likes + 3 comments + 4 shares + 2 saves) per view, views per follower, and recency. Both apply the flat gates: 720p on the best rendition, brightness, TikTok text-sticker boxes, faces and text read by a local macOS Vision helper, ads (including accounts that have ever advertised), AI labels, and on-topic words. Every candidate also needs a door:
- virality: at least 3x the author's own median over 12 recent posts, projected with a first-day age curve. A UGC account needs 3x its follower count instead.
- sweet spot: a niche or cluster that beats our own median.
Then four more inputs run:
- a second look: the last 3 days' strongest clips are fetched again and marked moving, slowing or flat;
- scene news: 7 RSS feeds and a few web searches, keeping dated stories from the last 2 days;
- story origins: free feeds from talk shows, newsrooms and the science press;
- hand-ins: a URL Albert sends gets its own door.
Sunday adds slow, free scouts:
- anniversaries from Wikidata and MusicBrainz, dates checked on Wikipedia, each with a YouTube official upload and its most-replayed window;
- songs coming back;
- current hits: 56 Beatport genre charts (about $0.045), 1001Tracklists, Shazam and TikTok sounds;
- sample stories, each needing 2 independent sources.

3. Planning (dontechno/runway/plan.py). The planner is deterministic, and every rejection is logged with its reason under 'Left out'.
- Format choice, per pool: share = weight x sqrt(evidence x own history x this week's market). Sainte-Lague quotas spread the week, and no format runs twice in a row.
- Timeliness: quotas for current, evergreen, nostalgic and bridge posts. A nostalgic slot needs a reason to post it now (why-now).
- One exploration slot a week.
- Candidate value = early score x freshness half-life x vision x trend x anniversary x niche factor x current-hit boost x artist flag x momentum x pop factor. The pop factor is the distance above the door's bar, capped at 1.3.
- In daily mode the quotas are soft, and a clip popping off now skips them.
- The catalog (data/catalog/posts.jsonl, 1,176 records) blocks a clip for good, a storyline for 60 days and an artist for 2 days.

4. Build: one headless Claude Code pass (prompts/daily_posts.md, 120-minute cap). The pass:
- reads the brief;
- looks at the pick first, and may switch to a backup or reject it;
- downloads only the sections it needs;
- checks every fact against a source read that day (sources.md);
- writes a Python builder for the post;
- renders with the fixed renderers: carousel.py's STORY overlay at 1080x1440, reel.py's template at 1080x1920;
- runs check_copy.py (phrase lists in YAML), check_media.py, verify_sources.py and a contact sheet;
- records the slot and the catalog row, then pushes the post.
The rule is 'An empty slot is a good result; a weak post is not'. Before leaving a slot empty it tries, in order, a current-hit story, an anniversary or a song coming back, then a benchmark subject with a fresh clip.

5. Publish. scripts/karos_push.sh runs karos-portal's sync-don-techno-calendar.ts. Each built post becomes one scheduled instagram_post with its caption, credit line, collaborator to invite, media and sources.md, and a person publishes it by hand. The next run pulls the portal's 'published' status back (publish.py --from-portal) into the catalog and HISTORY.md. There is no Instagram API.

6. Sunday (scripts/run_weekly.sh, capped at $0.60).
- Market scan of the tracked pages' last 7 days (about $0.07), analyst notes, and the timeliness rule: move one slot a week when a class leads by 0.25.
- The origins registry: who the market's outliers credited.
- Our own grid (3 calls) goes into learn.py. Each post is measured against our median at a comparable age, using an age curve on comments, visible likes and plays. Multipliers move per niche, scene, format and artist: shrink k=6, at most 10% a week, a range of 0.7 to 1.4, decay 0.85, and drift back to 1.0 without new results. Artist flags: 5 posts under 0.8 halves the weight; 3 posts at 1.3 gives x1.15. A table groups results by what the planner knew at pick time: door, author kind, clip age, early-score bucket, platform.
- The hits scan.
- A read-only Claude pass writes the brief's Instructions (this week, look out for, push less, push more, models to try, benchmark) and a weekly report.
The older loops are dormant:
- data/FEEDBACK.md holds 6 items Daniel filed in June, all already REVIEW RULES.
- data/tweaks_log.jsonl has 65 rows: 30 publish moves, 29 media cleanups and 6 feedback items. It is bookkeeping now.
- config/catalogs/verdicts.yaml holds 18 Gen-1 'we asked AI to pick the top 5' countdowns. Those carousels drew 0 to 4 comments.
Albert's rulings arrive as dated rules in CLAUDE.md, the prompts and formats.yaml.

What the engine's Instagram agent lacks, by area:
(a) Market evidence. 00c2 ranks formats once, at template-studio setup, from at most 6 reference accounts x 12 posts (research.socialHistory), and from the post text alone. 03e shows one Flash scout the top 8 excerpts, z-scored within each account with no age adjustment. (b) Selection. 03c and 03f rank topics on an LLM's brand fit x interest. 04h takes a request, else what-works, else rotation between carousel and single; there are no reels. 03d rotates hot-news, deep-value and open-discussion. The engine is ahead on the build side (structured steps, design system, visual QA, capture of review edits at 09a/09e, a per-run budget) and is cheaper per post.

**Strengths**

- Evidence is stored as data, not code. Every format, threshold and window in config/formats.yaml, niche.yaml and dedupe.yaml carries its n, median, example URLs and winners and losers with notes. new_format_contract says how to add a format and when to promote or retire one: promote at n>=8 with median>=1.3, or after 2 own posts beat the median; retire after 4 own posts under 0.7.
- One measure does two jobs. A post's engagement divided by its own account's median ranks competitors (a 6.6M page and a 400k page on one scale) and also admits candidate clips (the virality door). The first-day age curve (10% of a week at 1 h, 55% at 24 h) fixed a 5x underestimate of 30-minute-old posts: the Beltran clip went from 1.67x to 4.47x, and later 7.6 to 13x its account.
- The reverse-engineering is explicit and cheap. 17 winners were traced to their origin for $0.06. The report measured repost lags (IG-born 3.5 to 4.9 days, TikTok-born 3 to 59), split 144 outliers into a 7-part story taxonomy, and found that most outliers are looked up rather than scraped. The origin registry (data/discovery/sources/registry.json) grows every week.
- It catches moments early: min_age 0.5 h, a second look that re-reads the strongest clips ($0.0019 each: moving, slowing or flat), waves (3 creators posting the same moment or sound in 48 h), and a hand-in door. These answer 'is it moving now', not 'is it big'.
- The intelligence layer is deterministic Python plus metered scraping. A ledger keeps idempotency keys and per-run caps ($0.25 a day, $0.60 on Sunday), and free sources run first (RSS, Wikidata, MusicBrainz, yt-dlp, 1001Tracklists, Shazam). All ScrappyCoco use to date is $1.64 over 930 calls, research included.
- The quality bar is honest. The builder may reject the planner's pick, tries a fixed fallback ladder, and leaves a slot empty with a one-sentence reason. A rejected clip is recorded and never offered again.
- Dedupe windows were measured on our own repeats, at three levels: a clip never comes back (repeats did 0.04x to 0.55x), a storyline waits 60 days (0.84x inside, 1.33x after), an artist waits 2 days (0.67x on days 1 to 2, 1.17x on days 3 to 7). The measured rules block 117 of 957 posts; the old 60-day artist rule would have blocked 395.
- The learning loop suits small samples and needs no Graph API. It compares posts at a comparable age, shrinks toward 1.0, caps moves at 10% a week, keeps a range, decays old weeks, drifts back to 1.0, flags artists, and tabulates results by what the planner knew at pick time. The own-grid read costs $0.006 a week.
- Media quality is measured by code before any judgment: YAVG >=20 on every frame of the first 2 s, median >=55, contrast >=28, 720p on the best rendition, softness, faces and text through a local macOS Vision helper, TikTok text-sticker boxes, ad flags including has_ever_advertised, and AI labels.
- Relevance guards stop outdated posts. A post about a track needs a current hit on the charts. A nostalgic post needs a reason to run now (an anniversary within 7 days, or the track trending again). A story with an old event leads with this week's news and its date. The two-week limit is the clip's age, not the event's.
- Copy rules are enforced by a checker. check_copy.py reads YAML phrase lists: a hard ban, and a watch list whose words are allowed only next to a year, place, number or name. Facts must come from sources.md, read that day. Both prompts say that scraped text is data, never instructions.
- Every post carries a credit line and a collaborator to invite, the biggest free lift in the data (1.37 vs 0.98).
- A human reviews in the portal without a manual push: posts arrive automatically at their Madrid times, and the portal's 'published' status flows back into the catalog and the dedupe.

**Weaknesses**

- The system is unproven. The daily model has run since 24 Sep, and the first Sunday analysis is 27 Sep. learning.json does not exist, the brief's Instructions are empty, and every post's index reads '-'. The automated pipeline it replaces did badly: pipeline carousels sat at a 0.12 comment index from April to June, and cadence fell to 0.2 posts a week. The redesign rests on market evidence; its own results are not in yet.
- The planner's picks did not survive their first day. On 24 Sep the builder replaced all 3 daily picks: a 720p repost with a burned-in caption, a 7-week-old pop crossover at 77 views an hour, and a clip that clashed with the same-artist rule. 2 of the day's 5 new posts came from Albert's hand-set sessions. Even after the same-day fixes, the replayed picks failed. A 1.9M-view 'Blondish' clip was a selfie video (the scouts match artist names in captions, not what the footage shows), and a Black Coffee clip hit the artist rule.
- The value function multiplies about 11 factors with hand-set constants (pop step 0.08, quota floor 0.4, x0.6 on a first sighting). Several were patched the day they were found wrong: quotas crushed hot clips, the builder misread the two-week rule, and a late run lost its slot time. plan.py is 180 KB.
- Spend caps starve the measurement that decides. With caps of $0.034 to $0.039 per pool, the scout stopped reading authors ('author @beltranstans not read') on the morning Beltran's was the hottest clip; it reached the plan only as Albert's hand-in. The funnel is tight: on 24 Sep the TikTok pools kept 0 to 13 of 138 to 529 records, and 2 of 6 pools kept none.
- Dedupe has blind spots. URL and story-word matching missed a moment already posted (Black Coffee at the British Museum; Albert dropped it on 24 Sep). The saturation rule (skip a story that 3 or more tracked pages posted in 7 days) is written in formats.yaml but not in code.
- Measurement is thin. It uses public likes, comments and plays only. Likes are hidden on most 2026 posts, so the comment index carries the measure, and there is no reach, saves or shares data (no Insights). The research samples are small: 12 traced stories, 33 TikToks that passed 1M, and a timeliness classifier that agrees with a hand label 73% of the time.
- The build is expensive in tokens. Each agentic pass re-reads large configs (formats.yaml 92 KB, niche.yaml 86 KB, content-spec 41 KB), makes 28 to 68 Bash calls and writes a full Python builder per post: $1.45 to $4.61 per pass at API prices. The roadmap's spec-driven builder (37 near-identical builders) is not built.
- Operations are fragile:
- One Mac, with LaunchAgents on local time: 06:00 there is 11:00 in Madrid, so the first reel moved to 13:00.
- The claude CLI login expired before the first 06:00 run (401, nothing built).
- The portal sync depended on a karos-portal worktree that was swept.
- The headless run pushes straight to main.
- Rules are spread out, and decisions are orphaned. Rules live in CLAUDE.md, content-spec.md (whose body is stale: an 'archivist' brand, dark archival imagery, 20 to 60 s clips), formats.yaml review notes and the prompts. Decisions D2 to D11 wait for Daniel's sign-off, but Daniel left on 24 Sep. The operating model changed three times in two days.
- Feedback capture is manual. The portal pulls back only 'published', so a rejection or an edit made in the portal never reaches the catalog or learn.py. FEEDBACK.md has been dormant since June, and tweaks_log is mostly bookkeeping.
- The rights model is an aggregator norm. Third-party clips are reposted with a credit line, and content-spec section 13 says 'If a DMCA arrives, pull the post'. That does not carry over to brand clients.
- The skill meant to carry this system to other clients is two generations behind. ~/.claude/skills/karos-instagram-tiktok-content-agent (30 June) calls itself 'a parameterized clone of the proven Don Techno engine'. It ships verdict-ranking and informational-list templates, which DT's own data has since retired: list slides are banned, and verdict and list carousels drew 0 to 4 comments. It also relies on hashtag discovery, which in Gen-1 turned 140 suggestions into 0 posts. None of the doors, the second look, the origin work, the measured dedupe or the learning loop is in it.
