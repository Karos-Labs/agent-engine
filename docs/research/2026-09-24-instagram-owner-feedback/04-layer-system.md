# The layer system: judged design

Three readers mapped what exists across the portal, the middleware and the engine. Three designers then proposed a system from different angles (curated, statistical, agentic auditors), and a judge scored them and wrote one recommendation.

## Scores

- **1. Kits and DNA (curated guideline docs, one auditor skill, approval by PR)**: efficiency 8, correctness 6, fit 8, operability 9. Cheapest and most operable: 13 short docs, one loader, one skill and a storm runbook, riding the prompt pipeline that already exists (verified: the typed registry in scripts/prompt-registry.ts, check:prompts in quality.yml:178, publish-prompts.ts run by deploy-prep.yml:107 and deploy-prod.yml:102). Additive, fail-open, about 1% of a run's cost, no new service. Its weakness is correctness. The scale test is a raw share count (3 accounts and a third of the kit, top 15% vs bottom 30%) with no interval, no false-discovery control and no confirmation on fresh posts, computed on outlier lists the storm shows are contaminated (pinned posts sit at x3.7 the account median, paid at x0.68, giveaways at x16). The 'same line in 2 DNAs' rung for L2 can be one owner remark written twice. Counts live in prose (the Watching sections), so nothing can recompute or audit them. It leaves the middleware's designed home (config.craft_rules / craft.json) empty and puts client text in the engine repo; both are acceptable for month one only if line ids are chosen so a later import is a script. The weekly PR is both the latency and the bottleneck.
- **2. Pooled recipe model (hierarchical shrinkage over a closed recipe vocabulary)**: efficiency 6, correctness 9, fit 7, operability 5. The only design calibrated on the real storm; I re-ran loop-design/analyse.py and it reproduces the numbers (37 accounts with both formats, tau_account 0.38, tau_industry 0.16, platform carousel-vs-single x0.97 with a 95% interval spanning 1). Its simulations are the decisive evidence against the owner's literal ladder (about 14-23 false client rules per client from '3 winners', about 16 false industry rules from 2-client replication) and against Craft 11's outlier test. Exclusions, one vote per account, Hartung-Knapp intervals, FDR, discover-then-confirm, a prediction interval for 'consistent', propensity-logged experiments and promote/demote hysteresis are all right. Steady-state cost is low, but the build is the largest: about 8 new Postgres tables, a nightly estimator, a tagger, a capture endpoint, a projector, plus portal changes, and a six-person team with one technical support person must own a statistics job whose most common answer for months is 'not enough evidence yet'. Fit is good in principle (extends craft_rules, the C7 envelope, collect storing the record verbatim, the 90-day half-life in post-performance.ts, FunnelStageSchema in packages/core/src/types/run-input.ts) but the delta is wide. One claim looks wrong: scrappycoco.ts:137 does emit imageUrls, so 'socialHistory must first return image URLs' needs checking at the tool boundary.
- **3. Lesson Court (auditor panel per lesson, compiled post DNA with provenance)**: efficiency 6, correctness 7, fit 8, operability 6. Two ideas here beat anything in the other two. First, split every lesson into a mechanism (almost always L1, usually a gate or a parameterised renderer behaviour) and a value (brand kit, industry or client), so clients differ in parameters rather than rules and almost nothing has to climb the ladder: the owner's 14 letters become 5 gates, 3 onboarding capabilities, about 8 L1 defaults with kit-bound values, 1 industry rule and 0 client rules. Second, record per-choice provenance in the run so feedback on a post is blamed to its layer mechanically (its example is real: closer.html:1433 says every client's post closed on the same gradient panel, an L1 mechanism, not a Karos taste). Also right: norms vs drivers, a source-blind generality check, seed rows that reference constants so the seed cannot drift, decay by origin, reusing the 06 sheet as intake. Weaker on correctness than design 2: five Sonnet auditors plus an arbiter are non-deterministic judgment on thin evidence, the applicability score is a hand-tuned formula with magic bands, and the corpus test (best vs weak by likes + 3x comments, pinned excluded only) inherits the contamination design 2 measured. More moving parts than design 1 (5 tables, craft_rules columns, a court, a vocabulary steward) and about $0.15 of model spend per lesson on inputs that are mostly defects and constraints, which need no panel.

## Recommendation

Take design 1's shape (three layers as short, versioned, human-approved documents that ship through the engine's existing prompt pipeline; one auditor; kits on demand; standards separated from performance claims; defects to code) and correct its two weaknesses with the other designs. From design 2: the evidence behind every performance claim is arithmetic over a cleaned, tagged corpus with one vote per account, pooled at account, industry and platform scope with intervals, false-discovery control and a confirmation window; counts never live in prose. From design 3: every lesson is split into a mechanism and a value, and every run records which layer or parameter produced each choice, so the next piece of feedback finds its layer mechanically. Auditors are one rubric-driven skill plus a refuter for platform-wide and hard items, not a panel; a model call is used only to parse free text into the closed vocabulary; scope is decided by where evidence repeats, not by who spoke. Month one needs no database, no service and no UI: the recipe write-back on the run-state, a guidelines loader, 13 docs, a CI check, one tagging script and the auditor skill. The middleware's craft_rules/C7 channel stays the long-term home: line ids are minted in its id space now so the later import is a script, and the measured state (captures, tags, estimates) moves into middleware Postgres per D42 once the analytics leg actually runs in production.

## Layers

L1 platform: stays where it is, the Instagram prompts (instagram-copy@31 and the stage prompts) and the code gates (craft-hygiene, word budget, imagery floor, interest floor, value gate, karos-gates). A hard rule is code with a fixture; a default is prompt prose. L1 rules get ids only as the auditor touches them, and docs/guidelines/L1-index.md maps each dimension (design, structure, language, strategy, timing) to where its L1 lives. Owner approves every L1 change and every MUST. L2 industry: one kit doc per industry, ig-kit-<industry>, in agents/instagram-agent/prompts/, holding parameters in the engine's own enums (pictureDensity, format, seriesAffinity, postModes, plus declared-but-inert imageryRegister and coverForm until they have readers) and at most 25 one-line rules, each with a strength (MUST / DEFAULT / TRY), a dimension, a locale if any, and an evidence ref that CI resolves against the evidence store. The closed industry list is the set of kit files (six today: apparel-intimates, tech-news-media, startup-ecosystem, marketing-services, local-discovery-apps, fintech-investing); a new kit arrives with a client or a clear misfit, never as a taxonomy project. The kit id is the one industry key shared by portal (Client.category maps to it), middleware (learning_settings.sector becomes it) and engine (replaces CLIENT_SEGMENTS and PHOTO_FIRST_INDUSTRY for clients that have a DNA). Anna approves kits. L3 client: one DNA doc per client, ig-dna-<slug>, naming its kit, overriding the few parameters that differ, listing kit lines it skips with a reason, and holding at most 15 client lines. An explicit owner or client instruction about this client enters as DEFAULT at once; a performance pattern enters only as TRY. Brand kit, profile and the frozen visual direction are values the DNA points at, not rules. Locale is a qualifier on any line (the Hebrew register pack belongs to none of the three layers), not a fourth layer. Precedence at run time: hard lines, then run input, then operator client config, then learned preference (middleware preferences.formats), then DNA params, then kit params, then the platform default; between two conflicting prose lines the narrower wins; no line overrides a gate or the brand kit. Mechanism/value split throughout: a rule states the mechanism (for example 'devices take radius, fill and border from the client's tokens'), and the value comes from the brand kit, the kit params or the DNA params, so a client differs by parameters, not by rules.

## The auditor step

One auditor (a Claude Code skill in the engine repo with a versioned rubric, run each Monday on the week's inputs, after each research storm, and monthly to consolidate), plus a refuter instance only for L1 and MUST items, plus an end-of-batch Opus alignment review against the owner's lettered brief. Inputs: the owner's notes verbatim, 06 Agent Improve rows (the sheet gains Layer, Rule id and Verdict columns and is the owner/tester intake), client review actions and notes from the middleware feedback log, staff gate notes, ratings and templateFeedback, storm findings, and later performance surprises. Every input becomes an evidence item with an id, source, client, run, slide and quote, then one audited row, in this order. (1) Kind: defect (an existing requirement was not met: font not from the kit, logo not the kit file, cut-off quote, duplicate slide) becomes an engineering ticket plus a regression test and changes no prose; data fix (wrong client identity data, e.g. Kindly Yours' empty industry, Sitti's 'application') goes to the portal profile; constraint (legal, platform policy, legibility floor, brand integrity) becomes a hard gate, owner-confirmed, no statistics; preference (one client's taste) becomes an L3 DEFAULT for that client; hypothesis (a claim about what audiences respond to) continues; noise is recorded and changes nothing. (2) Blame by provenance: when the input names a run or slide, the run-state's recorded DNA provenance says which layer or parameter produced the criticised element; the default fix is at the source (the Karos gradient boxes blame the bundled closer template, an L1 mechanism, not a Karos rule). (3) Mechanism/value split and vocabulary: the lesson is restated as a mechanism plus a value, tagged with dimension, closed-vocabulary keys (the recipe vocabulary in packages/core/src/types/recipe.ts) and locale; a lesson with no key becomes a vocabulary-gap item for a human. One cheap model call (Haiku 4.5 with a strict schema, about $0.003) does the parse; near-duplicates merge into one lesson listing every source. (4) Generality, source-blind: before reading who said it, the auditor restates the lesson for three contrasting roster clients (say Hanky Panky, Geektime, XO Digital) and answers five written tests: another industry, another client in the same industry, depends on this brand's identity or offer or legal position, depends on a language or script, holds on this client's other platforms. That gives the candidate layer. (5) Standard vs performance claim: a standard (never, every post) keeps the scope its author states and is checked only for fit and conflicts; a performance claim gets only the scope its evidence reaches. (6) Evidence at scale, largest scope first: for a performance claim the auditor queries the evidence store for the mapped keys at platform, at every kit, and for this client, and applies the scale test below; the lesson lands at the widest layer where it is credible and consistent, otherwise at the narrower layer as TRY with a Watching entry at the wider one. If the data credibly point the other way against an owner statement, the numbers go back to the owner; the system never overrides him silently. (7) Strength: MUST names its enforcer (a gate, a workflow step id, or a ticket); DEFAULT meets its layer's bar; TRY waits for the experiment slot. (8) Extent per client, deterministic: a predicate gate first (locale, kit membership, photo supply, regulated, conflict with the brand kit or an existing L3 line), then for each affected client one of applies / applies adjusted (the DNA gets the adjusted line) / does not apply (the DNA gets a skip with the reason). For hypotheses on clients with at least 8 tagged posts per side, the client's own estimate can flip the call. Once the pooled model runs nightly (build later), this three-way call is replaced for performance claims by p = P(effect > 0 for this client), from the industry distribution when the client has no posts and moving toward its own evidence as posts accrue (about 0.4 weight at 5 posts per side, 0.8 at 30): p >= 0.8 applies, 0.6-0.8 lean, 0.4-0.6 not shown, < 0.4 automatic L3 override. (9) Conflicts and code fit: duplicate merges and adds evidence; conflict names the rule and the coupling (bigger type means the word cap follows from the minimum size); a line that asks for something a gate forbids is rejected; a line that needs a knob the engine lacks becomes a ticket and stays TRY. (10) Output: one PR per run holding the doc diffs, the audit table (one row per input: kind, mechanism, value, dimension, keys, layer, strength, per-client extent, evidence ref) and the tickets. Humans approve only constraints, new L1 defaults, L2 lines resting on remarks alone, vocabulary changes, owner-vs-data conflicts and retirement of owner-origin lines; everything else applies at the next publish.

## Scale test

The same test at every scope; only the breadth floor rises. Corpus: benchmark accounts per kit (12-20, category leaders plus 2-3 size-matched peers, 0.3x-30x the client's followers) and our clients' own accounts, tagged in the same closed vocabulary by the same tagger (Haiku 4.5 vision on the cover, a slides strip and the caption; API facts such as format, slides, caption length, hour, pinned, paid, collab and hidden-likes tagged deterministically; the tagger never sees engagement; kappa per dimension checked on the runs whose declared recipe is known, and any dimension below 0.6 is not pooled). Cleaning, with the storm figure behind each exclusion: pinned (x3.7 the account median), paid partnerships (x0.68), collabs, bait (comments above half the likes, giveaways, comment-for-asset CTAs; aerie's giveaway sat at x16.6), posts younger than 3 days (under a day they sit at x0.51 of settled; from day 2-3 within about 15%), and accounts with hidden likes switch to comments as the basis. Outcome per post: log(1 + likes + comments) minus the account's rolling median for the same format, winsorised to +/-1.5. Reels count only for the format dimension. Estimation: per account and level, the decay-weighted mean residual and its variance (one vote per account, the rule template-studio's rankReferenceFormats already applies); industry by random-effects pooling across accounts; platform by pooling across industries; Hartung-Knapp intervals because 5-15 accounts or 6 industries make normal intervals too narrow; the client's own account left out of its industry estimate. This is one SQL GROUP BY plus about 150 lines of plain arithmetic; month one it runs as a script over the storm's JSON (loop-design/analyse.py is the prototype). A level counts as general at a layer when it is credible (95% interval excludes 1.0, lift at least x1.15, passes BH-FDR at q = 0.10 across every level tested at that layer that night, and holds again in a second window with at least 30% new posts) and consistent (the 80% prediction interval for a new member of the layer excludes 1.0). Breadth floors: L2 needs at least 5 accounts each with at least 3 effective posts at the level (14 or more recommended; 14 x 30 posts detect a true x1.5 lift about 70% of the time, 25 x 30 about 92%); L1 needs at least 3 kits that each pass L2 plus the owner's sign-off. An L3 deviation needs the client to differ credibly from its industry-without-it with at least 8 own posts with the level and 8 without, confirmed in a second window. Norms are separate from drivers: a level used in at least 70% of posts by at least 70% of an industry's accounts is recorded as an L2 convention, a weak overridable default, never L1 (tech media posts 68% single images, local discovery 82% carousels, AI marketing and fintech 50-56% reels). A confounding check runs before any promotion: re-estimate the level jointly with the other dimensions inside each account; if the joint effect halves or flips, the level is reported as entangled and filed as a combined recipe rather than promoted. Working-posts phrasing for the owner: the share of a level among each account's top-quartile posts versus its other posts, pooled across accounts (Mantel-Haenszel), which is the same evidence stated as 'X% of top intimate-apparel posts put a person on the cover, against Y%'. A trait winners merely share is not a pattern, because common traits are shared by losers too. Storm evidence is attention evidence (likes and comments); once connected-account captures exist, our own posts add sends + saves per reach at 48h (early read) and 7d (decision), and no L3 rule may show a credible negative effect on that measure. What today's storm already says: format is a per-client choice (tau_account 0.38-0.39 vs tau_industry 0.14-0.16; platform carousel-vs-single x1.11, 95% interval x0.86-1.45), so no L1 or L2 format rule is supported and Craft 11's sector 'format lead' defaults should not be written.

## Post DNA assembly

The post DNA is resolved at run time and recorded, never hand-maintained as a separate artifact. A new checkpointed code step, 01a-read-guidelines, calls PromptStore.getPrompt('ig-dna-<clientSlug>'), reads the kit pointer from its header and calls getPrompt('ig-kit-<kit>'); a missing or malformed doc means today's behaviour plus a 'guidelines absent' readiness note, exactly like the C7 files. Parameters join the existing chain in 01-open-run as two rungs (DNA params, then kit params) below the learned preference and above defaultPictureDensityFor, which stays as the fallback for clients with no DNA; postTypeSource records 'client-dna' or 'industry-kit'; series affinity from the DNA or kit goes to seriesLibraryFor as a preferred list with CLIENT_SEGMENTS as the fallback. Prose lines become LearningCraft rules: MUST becomes kind hard, DNA lines are layer L3 and precede the kit's L2 lines, skipped kit lines are dropped, TRY lines are held back until the experiment slot exists, timing lines are kept for the calendar and never sent to the writer, evidence refs are stripped. mergeCraft() folds these into whatever craft.json the middleware projected (nothing today) and the existing craftRulesForPrompt renders the block into the copy input, the same field and wording x-craft, linkedin-craft and reddit-craft already document; instagram-copy@32 adds one short section saying hard lines win, then client, then industry, then the guide's defaults, no line overrides a gate or the brand kit, and the narrower of two conflicting lines wins. Every choice the run already makes (format/arm, series, visual system, picture density, hook pattern, CTA) records where its value came from ({layer, docVersion, lineId or param}); later a single chooseLevel(dna, dim, allowed, rng) helper takes over from preferredByPerformance and adds the experiment draw. At 10-write-run-state the run writes, as optional fields on the C7 record (RunStateRecordInputSchema is a z.object, so they must be added to the schema; its own header says an added optional field needs no version bump): guidelines {dna: 'ig-dna-hankypanky@2', kit: 'ig-kit-apparel-intimates@1'}, recipe (format, series, visualSystem, payloadKind, hookPattern, pictureDensity and its source, slideCount, imageSource, usedArrowBullets, skeleton signature, stage, vocabVersion 'ig-1'), provenance per parameter, rulesApplied, and assignment {policy: requested | default | variety | experiment, dim, level, propensity, seed}. The middleware's collect stores that record verbatim (upsert_run_record writes the jsonb whole), so record->'recipe' is queryable the day it ships without a migration, and the deliverable carries the same recipe so the portal can copy it into asset.meta.recipe and attach it to every capture. A person reads the DNA with npm run guidelines:show -- <slug>, which prints the same resolution the engine uses: resolved params with their source, then every line in precedence order with its layer and evidence. Later the middleware projects the same thing as context/learning/<platform>/post-dna.json inside the C7 envelope, with the client's per-level probabilities from the pooled model.

## Promotion and decay

Promotion raises proposals; a person approves at the top only. Into L3: an explicit owner or client instruction about this client is DEFAULT at once; a performance pattern is TRY only (trigger: at least 3 of the client's posts sharing a tag in its top third at 7 days, which is a hint, since three above-median posts share any one tag by chance about 1 in 8), and a TRY becomes DEFAULT once its experiment posts reach the client's 7-day median, or, once the pooled model runs, once the client's own estimate deviates credibly from its industry (8 posts per side, second window). L3 to L2: the same meaning in at least 2 DNAs of a kit (the owner's 2-3 clients) or corpus support at the L2 bar; the monthly consolidation moves the line into the kit and writes skips into the DNAs it does not fit. Because 5 of 6 industries have one client today, the replication unit is the account, not the client: benchmark accounts supply the L2 evidence now. L2 to L1: the same line supported in at least 3 kits (later half of all kits) with none contradicting, plus the refuter pass and the owner's approval; the line is then written into the platform prompt as a new version and removed from the kits. Demotion runs the other way: when an L1 default fails one industry, that kit gets an override line (the 3-5 picture band is the worked example: built from 5 B2B accounts, shipped fleet-wide, failed a lingerie client, patched with a regex; it belongs in the apparel kit as pictureDensity: photo-first). Priors from the owner, clients or research steer defaults and the experiment queue but never count toward promotion; only data promote. Decay by origin. Owner and client statements are policy: they do not age, they are superseded by a newer statement on the same key and scope, and retiring one needs a human. Research and pattern lines are re-tested at every storm; a line whose lift is no longer credible halves in weight and is retired after two failures. Measured lines use evidence discounting with the 90-day half-life already in post-performance.ts, with hysteresis: promote at 95%, demote to lean when the 80% interval includes 1.0, retire below x1.05 or when breadth fails twice; retired lines keep their history and reason and their ids are never reused. Craft 11's 'three later posts below median retires a rule' is dropped (it fires 1 in 8 on a rule that still works). Counted voice lessons keep the middleware's thresholds but are scoped to the platform they came from. Review dates and budgets as the backstop for authored lines: TRY 30 days, L3 90, L2 180, L1 365; contradiction (a later owner note, a storm where the pattern no longer holds, once metrics exist three posts following the line all below the client's 7-day median) triggers early review; a kit holds at most 25 lines, a DNA 15, each doc at most 3 TRY, so something must merge or retire before anything new goes in. Experiments (the owner's 1 in 10): every tenth scheduled run per client, deterministic by post number; manual runs never, because the client chose the content. Month one a TRY line is the experiment queue and reviewers judge those posts. Once the DNA carries a rate, each experiment randomises exactly ONE dimension with the propensity logged, and the whole fleet tests the same dimension for a month, chosen where the pooled posterior is widest, so about 20 randomised posts pool into one estimate; per-client experiments would never reach a usable sample. The seeded series and visual-system picks the engine already makes become partial experiments for free once their propensity is logged. Exploration keeps dropped levels sampled so a rule that stops working is caught.

## Where it lives

Engine repo (git, the existing prompt pipeline): L1 prompts and gates; ig-kit-<industry> and ig-dna-<slug> docs registered in scripts/prompt-registry.ts, checked by check:prompts, published to Firestore prompts/{id} by publish-prompts.ts on deploy-prep and on the manual prod promote; the recipe vocabulary (packages/core/src/types/recipe.ts, next to FunnelStageSchema so karos-ledger can import it); the guidelines loader (packages/workflow/src/primitives/guidelines.ts); the auditor skill (.claude/skills/guidelines-auditor/SKILL.md); evidence files (docs/guidelines/evidence/<date>-<source>.md) and the per-kit estimates.json summaries CI resolves evidence refs against. Line ids are minted in the middleware craft_rules id space (ig-kit-apparel-intimates.07) so the later import is a script, not a rewrite. GCS research/<storm-id>/: raw scraped posts, media, tags.jsonl, kept 180 days, linked from evidence files and never committed. Engine run-state (state/runs/<runId>.json) and the deliverable: recipe, guidelines versions, provenance, rulesApplied, assignment; collected verbatim into middleware run_state_records for runs the middleware dispatched. Middleware Postgres, per D42, as the long-term home for measured and derived state, built later: accounts, posts, post_captures (append-only, 48h and 7d windows are the captures nearest those ages), post_recipe_tags, model_runs, recipe_estimates, lesson_candidates (the single inbox), lessons; craft_rules extended with dimension, vocab_key, params, condition, locale, origin, lesson_id, evidence, weight, a proposed state and review_after; learning_settings.sector as a foreign key to the closed industry list; kit and DNA lines imported by id; post-dna.json and what-works.json projected through C7; L3 moves here (with a portal approval screen) at about 20 clients or when L3 changes daily, keeping the same line format. Portal: Firestore stays the display copy of metrics and sends every capture with its age to the middleware; asset.meta.recipe is copied from the deliverable; the 06 Agent Improve sheet is the owner/tester intake until lesson_candidates exists; Lessons, Industries and DNA views are internal (D34) and the client-facing 'what we learned' line never mentions review or approval. Never again: the engine or the portal writing context/learning files straight to GCS around the middleware (what-works.json is retired once the middleware emits it).

## What changes versus the owner's original plan

- Scope is decided by where evidence repeats, not by who spoke or where the remark was made. The auditor normalises every input into one lesson (kind, mechanism, value, dimension, vocabulary keys, locale, proposed layer) and a query against the evidence store answers the layer; the model is used only to parse free text. This is cheaper than a panel of judging agents and it fixes today's pattern, where every owner remark ships as L1 (instagram-copy is at v31 with 46 prompt commits since 1 Aug; v29-v31 each came from one client's post).
- Standards are separated from performance claims. The owner's quality, brand and legal bars keep the scope he states (arrows look like AI, so none anywhere) and never wait for replication; only 'this works' claims must repeat before they widen. That removes most of the evidence burden from his own feedback.
- Defects and constraints go to code, not prose. Most feedback on a post is about execution (font, logo, type size, lost words, duplicate slides); those become gates with fixtures and tickets, which apply to every client at once and never decay, instead of a new prompt version. Of the 14 points, only a handful are guidance at all.
- Every lesson is split into a mechanism and a value, and industry kits are parameter sets in the engine's own enums. Clients then differ by parameters, not by rules, so almost nothing has to climb the ladder, and a new client gets a complete DNA (kit params plus brand kit) at its first run with no per-client rule-writing.
- The literal ladder ('3 winners -> client rule; 2-3 clients -> industry; across industries + human -> platform') is replaced by one pooled test with a rising breadth floor. On the storm's real noise (within-account log SD about 1.06, 18-22% of posts above 2x the account median by chance) the literal rule writes about 14-23 false client rules per client and about 16 false industry rules via 2-client replication; the pooled test with FDR and a confirmation window writes about 0.1 false industry rules per refresh and finds a true x1.5 lift about 70% of the time with 14 accounts. The '3 winners' trigger survives only as a TRY hint.
- The replication unit is the account, not the client. Five of the six industries have exactly one client, so the 2-3-clients rung cannot fire for months; the 12-20 benchmark accounts per kit provide replication now and our clients' own accounts join the same pool.
- 'To what extent it applies to this client' becomes a computed call, not a judgment: a deterministic predicate gate plus applies / adjusted / skip per client now, and a probability P(effect > 0 for this client) that starts from the industry and moves toward the client's own evidence once the pooled model runs. A new client's extent for every active lesson is recomputed with no model call.
- Research runs per industry, not per client, so cost grows with about a dozen kits (under $1 per kit per month for scraping and tagging, about $10-15 a month all in) rather than with the client count; per-client competitors add L3 context only. Kits arrive on demand (six today) and the closed industry list is the set of kit files, which also becomes the one industry vocabulary shared by portal, middleware and engine (three unrelated ones exist today). This needs an explicit carve-out from D43.
- Fixed windows are needed only for our own posts: a 7-day decision with a 48-hour early read. Benchmark posts need one capture at least 3 days old, because the storm shows engagement has settled by then, so there is no re-scrape at fixed ages.
- The 1-in-10 experiments randomise one dimension per experiment, fleet-wide for a month, with the propensity logged; per-client experiments would never reach a usable sample. Month one they are simply the TRY lines, judged by the reviewer, and the engine's existing seeded variety picks become partial experiments for free.
- Decay is evidence discounting (the 90-day half-life already in code) with separate promote and demote bars, plus review dates and doc budgets for authored lines, and origin-based rules (owner statements are superseded, not aged). Craft 11's 'three later below-median posts retire a rule' is dropped because it fires 1 in 8 on a rule that still works.
- The post DNA is resolved at run time and recorded with per-choice provenance on the run-state, not a hand-maintained artifact; a print script shows it and a later nightly projection diffs it. Provenance is what lets the next piece of feedback find its layer mechanically.
- A locale qualifier is added to the model. Hebrew register rules belong to neither platform, industry nor client, and without the qualifier an auditor files them too narrow or too wide.
- Human approval shrinks to five decisions: constraints and hard gates, new L1 defaults, L2 lines resting on remarks alone, vocabulary changes, and owner-vs-data conflicts or retiring an owner-origin line. Everything else applies at the next publish, batched into one weekly PR that Anna reviews and one monthly storm PR.
- No second rule channel is built. Month one the docs are the rule surface and merge into the same craft block every agent already renders; their ids are minted in the middleware craft_rules id space, and the measured state moves into middleware Postgres per D42, so the owner's plan needs no new database, service or UI before the analytics leg is even scheduled in production.
